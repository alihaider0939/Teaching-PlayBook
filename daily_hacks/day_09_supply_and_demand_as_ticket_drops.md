# Day 9: Supply & Demand (Why Abstract Market Curves Bored Us & How Ticket Drops Fix It)

> **The Problem:** When economics classes start with *"The law of demand states an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, holding ceteris paribus,"* students immediately tune out. Drawing $P$ and $Q$ axes on a board makes microeconomics feel like dry math instead of real-world human behavior.
> **The Student Hack:** Reframe supply, demand, and equilibrium around trying to buy sold-out concert tickets or limited-edition sneaker drops.

> That intersection in the middle isn't just an abstract graph dot—it's the exact price where buyers and sellers agree on a trade without leaving empty seats or massive lines.

* **Textbook Way:** "Price determination occurs at the intersection of aggregate supply and demand curves. Surpluses drive prices down toward equilibrium, while shortages shift prices upward."
* **Peer Shortcut:** "Think of it like buying tickets for a massive concert drop:
  * **High Demand / Low Supply (The Resale Nightmare):** 50,000 people want tickets, but the venue only holds 5,000 seats. What happens? Resellers charge $1,000 per ticket because people are desperate.
  * **High Supply / Low Demand (The Empty Venue):** An artist books a stadium, but nobody shows up. Resellers sell $100 tickets for $10 outside the gate just to get rid of them.
  * **Equilibrium:** The exact ticket price where the stadium fills up completely, but no one is left standing outside screaming for a ticket."

---

## 🧠 Why Resale Market Metaphors Work

Gen Z lives in an economy dominated by drop culture, Ticketmaster queues, and secondary resale markets (e.g., StubHub, GOAT, eBay).

When you anchor economics to ticket queues:
1. **Shifts vs. Movement becomes obvious:** What happens if the artist goes viral on TikTok right before the concert? Demand shifts right instantly—more people want the same 5,000 seats, pushing resale prices up.
2. **Shortages & Surpluses are intuitive:** A shortage is just a virtual queue of 100,000 people waiting for 5,000 tickets. A surplus is a reseller sitting on unsold floor tickets 10 minutes after the show starts.
