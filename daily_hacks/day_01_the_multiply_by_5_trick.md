# Day 1: The "Scale Up, Cut Down" Math Trick

> **The Problem:** Students slow down and zone out when doing multi-digit multiplication by 5 because traditional long multiplication creates unnecessary mental friction.
> **The Student Hack:** Never multiply by 5. Add a zero, then cut the number in half.

---

## 💡 How a Student Explains $48 \times 5$ to Another Student

If I'm sitting next to a friend during a test and they get stuck on $48 \times 5$, I’m not going to tell them to set up vertical multiplication and carry the 4. That takes too long and wastes mental energy.

* **Textbook Way:** "Write $48$, put $5$ underneath. $8 \times 5 = 40$, write $0$, carry the $4$. $4 \times 5 = 20$, add $4$ to get $24$. Answer is $240$."
* **Peer Shortcut:** "Bro, don't multiply by 5. Put a zero on the end to make it $480$, then cut it in half. Half of $480$ is $240$. Done."

One method feels like tedious work; the other feels like a system loophole. When you teach us the loopholes first, we actually want to engage.

---

## 🧠 Quick Examples

* **$14 \times 5 \rightarrow$** Add zero ($140$), cut in half $\rightarrow \mathbf{70}$
* **$84 \times 5 \rightarrow$** Add zero ($840$), cut in half $\rightarrow \mathbf{420}$
* **$35 \times 5 \rightarrow$** Add zero ($350$), cut in half $\rightarrow \mathbf{175}$
